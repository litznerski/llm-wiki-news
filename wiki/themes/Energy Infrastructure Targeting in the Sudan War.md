# Energy Infrastructure Targeting in the Sudan War

> [!NOTE] Strategic Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: Deliberate military doctrine utilizing long-range unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to strike oil export pipelines, pumping stations, and electrical distribution grids to inflict economic strangulation, degrade state logistics, and compel political concessions.
> - **Primary Case**: Repeated October 2026 RSF drone strikes on the [[Haiya]] oil pumping station and electrical substation in [[Red Sea State]].

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

As conventional ground operations became bogged down in trench warfare and static sieges, the [[Rapid Support Forces]] expanded the use of long-range loitering munitions and strike drones into government-controlled eastern and northern states. The doctrine shifted from purely tactical battlefield interdiction to strategic infrastructure attritional warfare, deliberately striking electrical substations, water treatment facilities, and crude oil pipelines to paralyze state revenues, collapse urban municipal services, and undermine civilian morale in SAF-controlled safe havens.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Oil infrastructure in Sudan represents both the primary sovereign revenue stream for the Port Sudan-based government and the economic lifeblood of neighboring South Sudan, which relies on the export pipeline to the Bashayer marine terminal. Throughout the conflict, controlling and taxing oil transit has been a key source of revenue leverage. When negotiations stalled, physical infrastructure became an asymmetric target for UAV strikes.

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **Eastern Corridor Strikes**: In late October 2026, RSF drones struck the crude oil pumping station and electrical substation in [[Haiya]] (`SD10002`), setting crude oil storage tanks on fire.
- **River Nile & Northern Penetration**: On 30 October 2026, an RSF suicide drone strike destroyed the transformer substation in [[Shendi]] (`SD08004`), knocking it out of service and setting fire to a [[Nile Petroleum Company]] fuel depot.
- **White Nile Grid Blackout**: On 26 October 2026, RSF drones destroyed the control room of the Mashkoor transformer substation in [[White Nile State]], cutting power to [[Ad Douiem]], El Kawa, and Wad az Zaki and tripling basic commodity prices.
- **Systemic Power Grid Degradation**: Over 13 electricity distribution stations across Gedaref, Khartoum, Al Jazirah, Red Sea, Sennar, White Nile, River Nile, Northern State, and South Kordofan were disabled by drone strikes, precipitating catastrophic nationwide blackouts.
- **Urban Utility Targeting**: In [[El Obeid]], water pumping stations and municipal power networks were knocked out, leaving hundreds of thousands of civilians without potable water.

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **Water & Hospital Collapse**: Disrupting electrical grids immediately disables hospital intensive care units, dialysis centers, and cold-chain vaccine storage, multiplying indirect civilian mortality.
- **Regional Friction with South Sudan**: Threatening the oil pipeline endangers South Sudan's macro-economic solvency, heightening regional geopolitical instability.
- **Violations of IHL**: The UN Secretary-General and human rights bodies have reiterated that deliberate attacks on critical infrastructure indispensable to civilian survival constitute grave violations of international humanitarian law.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

Belligerents justify infrastructure strikes by alleging that power substations and pumping facilities power enemy command posts, drone control centers, and military workshops. International humanitarian legal experts emphasize that the principle of proportionality prohibits attacks when anticipated civilian harm excessively outweighs direct military advantage.
