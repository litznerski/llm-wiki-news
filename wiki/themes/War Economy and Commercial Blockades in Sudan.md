# War Economy and Commercial Blockades in Sudan

> [!NOTE] Strategic Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: Deliberate military interdiction of civilian commercial transport, predatory toll checkpoints, and fuel/grain supply strangulation utilized by conflict actors as economic siege weapons, driving hyperinflation and acute food insecurity.
> - **Primary Case**: RSF drone strikes on commercial trade trucks in [[El Malha]], rampant consumer commodity price inflation in [[Netiga]], and agricultural irrigation failure leading to famine warnings in [[River Nile State]].

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

The Sudan conflict has produced a predatory war economy characterized by the weaponization of trade routes, systemic extortion at checkpoints, and the kinetic interdiction of food and fuel convoys. Ground supply corridors connecting agricultural zones and seaports to consumer markets in Darfur and Kordofan are fragmented into extortionate toll regimes. When belligerents fail to secure political capitulation through conventional combat, they enforce commercial blockades or target merchant transport to starve opposing territorial enclaves, destroying market arbitrage and inducing artificial famine.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Before the war, Sudan relied heavily on domestic wheat production in northern riverine irrigation schemes and sorghum cultivation across central plains, balanced by grain imports through Port Sudan. Following the outbreak of hostilities, the national banking system severed inter-regional transactions, while the crash of the Sudanese pound and localized cash shortages forced populations into informal barter and inflated currency premiums. In RSF-controlled western territories, military commanders established parallel tax regimes and monopolized fuel smuggling corridors from Libya and Chad.

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **Targeted Kinetic Interdiction of Supply Convoys**: On 1 November 2026, long-range drones struck commercial vehicles along the northern desert corridor in [[El Malha]] (`SD02008`), destroying three commercial trucks laden with basic foodstuffs and terrorizing commercial drivers.
- **Runaway Food Inflation in Enclosed Markets**: In [[Netiga]] (`SD03004`) in South Darfur, the severance of trade routes from Kordofan and White Nile caused flour and cooking oil prices to double within 72 hours, precipitating acute malnutrition among displaced families.
- **Utility Sabotage and Secondary Hyperinflation**: In [[Ad Douiem]] (`SD16003`) in [[White Nile State]], the destruction of the Mashkoor electrical substation tripped ice prices from 10,000 to 30,000 SDG, collapsed bakery bread output, and spawned an informal fee-based solar device charging trade.
- **Commercial Encirclement of Neutral Havens**: In East [[Jebel Marra]], the [[Dar Omo]] circle and [[Liba]] suffered near-total agricultural failure and a 10-month medical clinic outage under an encircling RSF lowland commercial siege, putting 14,000 residents and IDPs at immediate risk of famine.
- **Agricultural Sector Strangulation in the North**: In [[River Nile State]], electricity blackouts and fuel price spikes halted electric and diesel irrigation pumps, causing the [[National Umma Party]] to declare a state of acute famine emergency.
- **Arbitrary Taxation and Road Closures**: Both SAF intelligence organs and RSF field brigades operate hundreds of arbitrary checkpoints where commercial vehicles must pay multi-million pound extortion fees to pass.

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **IPC Phase 5 Catastrophe Acceleration**: Commercial blockades and agricultural collapse are direct drivers pushing millions into IPC Phase 4 (Emergency) and IPC Phase 5 (Catastrophe) across Darfur, Kordofan, and Khartoum State.
- **Depletion of Livelihoods & Coping Strategies**: Families are forced to sell livestock, productive agricultural assets, and land at fire-sale prices, locking populations into generational poverty.
- **Smuggling Networks and Borderland Profiteering**: Illicit cross-border trade networks traversing Chad, Libya, and South Sudan have empowered warlords and non-state armed actors, eroding state institutions.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

Belligerents justify checkpoints and supply interdiction by asserting that commercial vehicles carry dual-use goods, weapons, or military rations to enemy troops. However, economic monitors, UN agencies, and civil society groups emphasize that the vast majority of interdicted cargo consists of civilian staples—flour, sugar, edible oils, and medical consumables—demonstrating that starvation and economic strangulation are intentionally wielded as instruments of warfare.
