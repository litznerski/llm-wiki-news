# Regional Proxy Warfare and Transnational Drone Supply Networks in Sudan

> [!NOTE] Structural Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: The externalization of the Sudan conflict through transnational weapons pipelines, illicit logistics corridors, and state-sponsored drone technologies transferring advanced systems to the [[Rapid Support Forces]] (RSF) and [[Sudanese Armed Forces]] (SAF).
> - **Key External Actors**: United Arab Emirates, Iran, Russia/Wagner, Chad, Libya (Haftar), and Ethiopia.

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

The duration and lethality of the Sudan war are fundamentally sustained by external regional sponsors who supply advanced weaponry, unmanned aerial vehicles, satellite guidance technology, and mercenary personnel. Rather than a localized civil conflict, the war operates as a high-stakes proxy battlefield where regional Middle Eastern and African powers compete for Red Sea maritime access, agricultural land, gold wealth, and regional geopolitical dominance, circumventing the UN arms embargo on Darfur.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Sudan's military-industrial complex was historically tied to Iranian and Russian technology transfers, which supplied the SAF with domestic drone production capabilities (Yarmouk and Giad facilities). Conversely, the RSF leveraged its lucrative artisanal gold-mining monopoly in Darfur to establish deep commercial and security partnerships with the [[United Arab Emirates]], financing private charter flights, shell companies, and transnational procurement routes through the Sahel and Horn of Africa.

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **Western Influx via Chad**: Cargo aircraft flights landing at Amdjarass airport in eastern Chad transferring weapons, ammunition, and medical aid that cross into Darfur under humanitarian pretexts.
- **Southeastern Corridor & Assosa Accusations**: In October 2026, the Sudanese Foreign Ministry formally charged that the [[Assosa]] base in Ethiopia was utilized by regional sponsors (principally the UAE) to supply the RSF and launch long-range UAVs against eastern infrastructure.
- **SAF Counter-Supply**: The SAF bolstered its aerial capabilities through procurement of Iranian Mohajer-6 and Russian-supplied reconnaissance and strike UAVs, launching long-range strikes against RSF-held cities such as [[Nyala]] and [[El Obeid]].

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **Lethality Multiplier**: Advanced UAVs with thermal optics and GPS guidance enable strikes against crowded urban markets and displacement camps far behind frontlines, making drones the primary driver of civilian deaths in 2026.
- **Regional Destabilization**: Friction between Sudan and neighboring Chad and Ethiopia threatens wider regional escalation and complicates multilateral peace talks.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

While foreign states consistently deny direct military involvement—insisting shipments represent humanitarian aid or sovereign defense arrangements—independent reports by the UN Panel of Experts, the UN Fact-Finding Mission (A/HRC/63/23), and Amnesty International have forensically documented serial numbers, flight logs, and satellite imagery confirming the continuous flow of foreign arms to both sides.
