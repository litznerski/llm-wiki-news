# Multilateral Capture and Accountability Politics in Sudan

> [!NOTE] Structural Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: The diplomatic maneuvers by external conflict sponsors—predominantly the [[United Arab Emirates]]—to achieve leadership influence and executive control within multilateral bodies like the [[United Nations]], thereby insulating proxy forces from international sanctions and justice.
> - **Primary Case**: The international civil society backlash against the UAE's nomination of [[Lana Nusseibeh]] for UN Secretary-General in October 2026.

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

External authoritarian sponsors of armed conflicts utilize multilateral funding, institutional candidacies, and diplomatic leverage to insulate themselves and their proxy allies from international accountability. When powerful regional patrons seek executive leadership posts within international institutions, civil society organizations warn of "autocratic capture"—a dynamic wherein the very multilateral bodies mandated to protect civilians and investigate war crimes risk having their independence compromised by governments accused of enabling those violations.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Wealthy Gulf states have expanded voluntary funding and diplomatic footprint across the UN system, serving on human rights bodies, leading specialized agencies, and hosting major international summits. In the Sudan context, the UAE's non-permanent tenure on the UN Security Council (2022–2023) was characterized by repeated defenses of its foreign policy and dismissals of UN panel reports documenting arms flows to the RSF.

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **The 2026 UNSG Candidacy**: The UAE's nomination of former UN ambassador [[Lana Nusseibeh]] to succeed António Guterres as UN Secretary-General triggered unprecedented resistance from Sudanese diaspora networks, international human rights monitors (Human Rights Watch, Avaaz, Protection Approaches), and women's rights groups (SIHA Network).
- **Denial of Atrocities**: Critics cited Nusseibeh's record of blanket denials regarding UAE military aid to the RSF, arguing that elevating an Emirati diplomat would constitute a symbolic and operational erasure of the atrocities committed in [[El Fasher]] and elsewhere.
- **Transitional Justice Compromise**: Human Rights Watch chief advocacy officer [[Bruno Stagno Ugarte]] warned that dismissing UN findings undermines justice and sets a dangerous precedent of institutional complicity.

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **Erosion of Public Trust**: Sudanese community leaders noted that electing a candidate from a state arming the RSF would cause civilians and victims of international crimes to completely lose faith in the UN's moral and legal authority.
- **Litmus Test for Global Justice**: Civil society mobilization forced the issue of Sudan's war into the center of the UN Secretary-General selection process.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

Candidate Lana Nusseibeh defended her candidacy by highlighting her leave of absence from the UAE government, describing RSF crimes as "atrocious," and pledging full support for UN fact-finding missions and transitional justice. Civil society leaders maintain that diplomatic rhetoric cannot substitute for structural independence from a state actively accused of violating the UN arms embargo.
