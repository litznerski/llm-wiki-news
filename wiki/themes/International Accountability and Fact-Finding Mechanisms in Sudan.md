# International Accountability and Fact-Finding Mechanisms in Sudan

> [!NOTE] Structural Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: The establishment, evidence-gathering methodologies, and multilateral resistance surrounding international forensic investigations and legal accountability mechanisms for war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocidal acts in Sudan.
> - **Primary Organ**: The [[Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan]] established by [[UN Human Rights Council]] resolution A/HRC/RES/54/2.

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

The collapse of domestic judicial accountability in Sudan following the April 2023 conflict necessitated the intervention of multilateral investigative mandates. The [[UN Human Rights Council]] created the [[Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan]] to collect, consolidate, and analyze forensic evidence of violations of international humanitarian law and human rights abuses, identifying responsible commanders and supply networks to support future criminal proceedings at the International Criminal Court (ICC) and national jurisdictions under universal jurisdiction.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Sudan has an extensive history of international criminal investigations dating back to UN Security Council Resolution 1593 (2005), which referred the Darfur situation to the ICC, culminating in arrest warrants for Omar al-Bashir and Ali Kushayb. When conflict erupted in 2023, impunity among senior military and paramilitary leadership created a structural barrier to justice, leading civil society to lobby for an independent UN investigative mechanism endowed with robust forensic protocols.

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **El Fasher Genocide Determinations**: In February 2026, the FFM published landmark findings concluding that the RSF assault on [[El Fasher]] exhibited the hallmarks of genocide against Fur and Zaghawa populations.
- **External Arms Supply Tracking**: In September 2026, the Mission published report A/HRC/63/23 exposing transnational weapon networks, drone transfers, and foreign fighter logistics sustaining the warring factions.
- **Urban Encirclement & Detention Inquiries**: In October 2026, the Mission detailed severe repression, arbitrary detention, torture, and drone warfare in [[El Obeid]] (`A/HRC/65/CRP.1`), highlighting the weaponization of Security Cells and summary executions in [[Um Badr]] ([[2026-09-24 Um Badr Market Summary Executions]]).
- **Proposal for Specialized Independent Judicial Mechanism**: In A/HRC/65/CRP.1 (Para 84), the FFM formally recommended the creation of a specialized independent international or hybrid judicial mechanism for Sudan to prosecute perpetrators of war crimes and crimes against humanity, operating in tandem with the ICC ([[2026-10-28 UN FFM Recommendation for Specialized Independent Court for Sudan]]).

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **Deterrence & Evidence Preservation**: Secures digital, testimonial, and satellite evidence under the Berkeley Protocol for future international tribunals.
- **Diplomatic Polarization**: Both the SAF-led government in Port Sudan and external sponsors (such as the UAE) have engaged in intense diplomatic lobbying to undermine, discredit, or obstruct the renewal of the FFM's mandate.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

While human rights advocates argue that international justice is vital to prevent future atrocities, sovereign actors often argue that external indictments hinder immediate ceasefire negotiations. FFM experts maintain that sustainable peace is impossible without holding perpetrators of mass atrocities accountable.
