# State Surveillance and Civic Space Contraction in Sudan

> [!NOTE] Structural Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: Systematic contraction of civic space, criminalization of civil society, and arbitrary detention by warring parties—predominantly the [[Sudanese Armed Forces]] (SAF) and military intelligence in government-controlled areas—to suppress dissent, neutral civilian aid networks, and independent reporting.
> - **Primary Documentation**: Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan report on El Obeid (A/HRC/65/CRP.1, October 2026).

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

As the Sudan conflict hardened into territorial stalemate, both belligerents increasingly viewed independent civil society, pro-democracy advocates, and humanitarian volunteers as existential security threats. In government-held garrison cities such as [[El Obeid]], [[Port Sudan]], and [[Atbara]], SAF military intelligence and affiliated security cells instituted pervasive mass surveillance and arbitrary detention regimes. This structural suppression aims to eliminate civilian monitoring of war crimes, silence calls for an end to the war, and enforce total political obedience through fear, incommunicado imprisonment, and torture.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Sudan's post-colonial authoritarian governance relied historically on the National Intelligence and Security Service (NISS)—rebranded as the General Intelligence Service (GIS)—to dismantle labor unions, student movements, and legal associations. Following the 2021 military coup and the outbreak of war in April 2023, wartime emergency decrees revived these security apparatuses with expanded powers of warrantless arrest and incommunicado detention, specifically targeting members of the [[Sudanese Resistance Committees]] and [[Emergency Response Rooms]].

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **El Obeid Security Cells**: In [[El Obeid]] (`SD02001`), the UN Fact-Finding Mission documented that thousands of civilians, lawyers, trade unionists, and aid workers were detained by SAF security cells in unofficial, secret detention centers as well as the El Obeid Central Prison.
- **Torture & Abuse**: Verified findings documented torture, ill-treatment, deaths in custody, and credible reports of sexual violence against female detainees.
- **Transnational Repression Across Borders**: In Section III of `A/HRC/65/CRP.1`, the Mission revealed that intimidation and surveillance extend beyond Sudan's borders into Chad, Kenya, Uganda, and the United Kingdom. Sudanese human rights defenders in the diaspora faced physical assault at public events in the UK, while prominent exiled pro-democracy writers were targeted with online death threats and assassination calls.
- **Reciprocal RSF Abuses**: In RSF-held territories (Darfur and Al Jazirah), the paramilitary force operated widespread abduction, extortion, and forced disappearance networks targeting local community leaders.

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **Evisceration of Grassroots Aid**: By arresting volunteers and humanitarian workers, the repression cripples the only functioning grassroots mutual-aid networks delivering food and medical relief.
- **Destruction of Peace Potential**: As noted by UN expert [[Mona Rishmawi]], shrinking civic space undermines public accountability and severely jeopardizes prospects for any meaningful, inclusive Sudanese dialogue to end the war.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

State authorities frame these crackdowns as counter-espionage measures aimed at suppressing RSF "fifth columns" and coordinating military defense during urban encirclement. Human rights bodies and international monitors conclude that these practices constitute systematic violations of international human rights law and international humanitarian law that may amount to war crimes.
