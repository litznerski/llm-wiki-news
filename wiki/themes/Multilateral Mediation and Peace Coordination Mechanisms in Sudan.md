# Multilateral Mediation and Peace Coordination Mechanisms in Sudan

> [!NOTE] Structural Mechanism Anchor ("Why")
> - **Core Dynamic**: The structural fragmentation, diplomatic synchronization efforts, and persistent obstacles characterizing international peace initiatives to achieve a cessation of hostilities and civilian transition in Sudan.
> - **Primary Mechanism**: The *Consultative Meetings on Enhancing Coordination of Peace Initiatives and Efforts in Sudan* convened in [[Geneva]], Switzerland.

---

## 1. Core Thesis & Structural Mechanism ("Why")

Since April 2023, diplomatic initiatives seeking to end the war have suffered from severe forum shopping, geopolitical competition among regional sponsors, and a total lack of coordination. Multiple competing mediation tracks—the US-Saudi Jeddah Platform, the African Union/IGAD roadmap, Egypt's Neighboring States initiative, Manama secret talks, and the Geneva consultations—have allowed the warring parties to exploit diplomatic fragmentation, evading compliance with humanitarian agreements while continuing battlefield escalation.

---

## 2. Historical Roots & Political Economy Anatomy

Mediation efforts historically faced structural resistance from Sudan's entrenched military elite, who reject civilian oversight, and regional powers pursuing rival economic and strategic interests along the Red Sea and Nile Basin. When direct talks between SAF and RSF broke down repeatedly over mutual demands for total disarmament and troop redeployment, mediators established external consultative coordination platforms to align international leverage.

---

## 3. Operational Manifestations Across Frontlines

- **Geneva Consultations (October 2026)**: Co-hosted by the [[United Nations]] and Switzerland, nearly two dozen nations convened to synchronize peace tracks and address the escalating "drone war."
- **The Quad & Quintet Tracks**: Mediation involves the Quad (United States, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt) and Quintet (AU, IGAD, UN, EU, Arab League) to coordinate pressure on external weapons pipelines.
- **Exclusion of Belligerents**: Recent coordination rounds intentionally excluded direct SAF and RSF delegations to allow third-party mediators to align positions without public grandstanding.

---

## 4. Humanitarian, Regional & Diplomatic Consequences

- **Unfulfilled Commitments**: Despite high-level diplomacy, ground realities remain defined by famine, mass displacement, and intensifying drone strikes on civilian infrastructure.
- **Civilian Protection First**: Swiss State Secretary [[Alexandre Fasel]] affirmed in Geneva that international humanitarian law requires the protection of non-combatants and aid delivery cannot be held hostage to a political settlement.

---

## 5. Empirical Debates, Uncertainties & Competing Analyses

Critics argue that external consultative meetings without the physical participation or enforcement power over belligerents represent diplomatic theatre. Conversely, UN envoys such as [[Pekka Haavisto]] maintain that building unified international consensus and shutting down external weapons flows are essential prerequisites before direct talks can succeed.
