## 1. 5W1H Source Intelligence Summary & Provenance

On 1 November 2026, [[Radio Dabanga]] recorded dramatic price inflation for staple food crops and grocery provisions across commercial markets in **[[Netiga]]** (`SD03004`) locality, [[South Darfur]]. The hyperinflation coincided with a steep collapse in livestock prices, severely depressing the purchasing power of pastoralists and subsistence farmers.

Specific market prices recorded in Netiga include: a sack of millet (*dukhn*) reached **400,000 Sudanese Pounds (SDG)**; sorghum (*dura*) hit **380,000 SDG**; peanuts reached **150,000 SDG**; a jerrycan of cooking oil surged to **300,000 SDG**; a 25-kilogram bag of imported flour climbed to **175,000 SDG**; and rice reached **155,000 SDG**. Resident Abdel Hadi Mohammed Hamed attributed the price spikes to poor rainfall, localized agricultural failure, and the drying up of pastures, which forced desperate herders to dump cattle on illiquid markets.
