# 2026-10-28 UN HRC FFM: Sudan's Unrelenting War - Civilian Devastation and Deepening Insecurity in El Obeid (A/HRC/65/CRP.1)

## 1. 5W1H Source Intelligence Summary & Provenance

> [!NOTE] Source Provenance & Admiralty Assessment
> - **Issuing Authority**: [[Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan]] (FFM Sudan), mandated by the [[UN Human Rights Council]] pursuant to resolution 62/1 (adopted 6 July 2026 following an urgent debate on 3 July 2026).
> - **Document Reference**: United Nations General Assembly Document `A/HRC/65/CRP.1` (Conference Room Paper, 65th Session of the Human Rights Council, dated 28 October 2026).
> - **Evidentiary Standard & Methodology**: Standard of proof applied is "reasonable grounds to believe", established through 29 direct witness and victim interviews, expert consultations in Nairobi (5 June 2026) and London (17–19 August 2026), commercial satellite imagery analysis, forensic video geolocation, and corroborated open-source data under the Berkeley Protocol.
> - **Admiralty Scale Rating**: **`A1`** (`reliability_score: 0.95`). Highest institutional credibility, completely independent investigative mandate, verified multi-source corroboration, and neutral evidentiary standard.

### 5W1H Extraction Ledger
- **Who**: Authored by the UN Fact-Finding Mission (Chair [[Mohamed Chande Othman]], members [[Mona Rishmawi]] and [[Joy Ngozi Ezeilo]]). Document covers military operations, human rights violations, and potential war crimes committed by the [[Sudanese Armed Forces]] (SAF) 5th Infantry Division, allied Islamist militias ([[Al-Baraa Ibn Malik Brigade]]), mobilized auxiliaries (*mustanfireen*), and the [[Rapid Support Forces]] (RSF), as well as foreign actors (Egyptian military airstrikes in border mining areas) and allied armed groups ([[Sudan People's Liberation Movement-North (al-Hilu)]]).
- **What**: Comprehensive 19-page investigative conference room paper documenting acute civilian devastation, systematic RSF drone warfare against populated residential quarters and infrastructure, SAF co-location of military assets in civilian neighborhoods, pervasive arbitrary detention and social control by the SAF "Security Cell" (*Al-Khaliyya al-Amniyya*), systemic torture and cholera deaths in El Obeid Central Prison, summary executions of 25 bound detainees in Um Badr, expanding secondary fronts in Blue Nile State, and transnational repression of the Sudanese diaspora.
- **When**: Reporting period covers June 2026 through October 2026 (formally shared with the Republic of the Sudan on 26 October 2026, officially published 28 October 2026).
- **Where**: Primary geographic locus is [[El Obeid]] (`SD02001`) and [[North Kordofan]] (`SD02`), with crucial investigative findings extending to [[Um Badr]], [[Blue Nile State]] ([[Kurmuk]], Geissan, Wad al-Mahi, Um Darfa), the [[Abyei]] Demilitarized Area (Amiet Market), and the Sudan-Egypt border zone (Jabal al-Uqaydat).
- **Why**: Demonstrates how changing frontlines and the partial reopening of the Khartoum–El Obeid highway in July 2026 failed to secure civilian protection; illustrates how the RSF shifted to terrorizing civilian centers through long-range loitering munitions, while the SAF apparatus weaponized emergency laws and arbitrary detention as instruments of social control and suppression of civic space.
- **How**: RSF conducted coordinated drone strikes, repeat double-tap strikes, and targeted essential municipal water and power grids; SAF Military Intelligence deployed joint Security Cells conducting warrantless arrests, incommunicado detention in sub-human conditions (35–40 persons in 2.5×2m cells), coercive interrogation, sexual harassment, and torture.

## 3. Bilingual Direct Citations & Forensic Evidence

> [!QUOTE] 1. Legal Qualification of RSF Drone Warfare as War Crimes
> **English Verbatim (A/HRC/65/CRP.1, Section IV, Para 84):**
> > "The mission has reasonable grounds to believe that the Rapid Support Forces conducted drone strikes, in populated areas of El Obeid, resulting in multiple deaths and injuries, as documented in section II.C above. Repeated strikes affected residential neighbourhoods, educational facilities, locations where civilians had gathered or sought shelter, and civilian infrastructure, including fuel stations and electricity infrastructure. The manner in which drone strikes are taking place violates the obligations of the Rapid Support Forces to respect the principles of distinction, proportionality and precautions in attack. Such violations may amount to war crimes."
>

> [!QUOTE] 2. Legal Qualification of SAF Custodial Torture and Inhumane Detention
> **English Verbatim (A/HRC/65/CRP.1, Section IV, Para 89):**
> > "The mission finds that the detention practices applied by the Sudanese Armed Forces in El Obeid amount to serious violations of international humanitarian law, including violence to life and person and outrages upon dignity, as well as violations of international human rights law, including the prohibition of torture and other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment, and the right to life, liberty, security of person, health and adequate food. Such conduct may amount to war crimes, particularly cruel treatment, torture, and outrages upon personal dignity."

> [!QUOTE] 3. Forensic Documenting of Um Badr Summary Executions
> **English Verbatim (A/HRC/65/CRP.1, Section II.D, Para 37):**
> > "Further northwest, in Um Badr, North Kordofan, approximately 270 kilometres from El Obeid, the mission verified footage reportedly recorded on 24 September showing the execution of approximately 25 persons accused of looting Um Badr market. The blindfolded victims, whose hands were bound, appeared to have been executed while in the custody or under the control of members of the Rapid Support Forces. The summary execution of persons in custody is prohibited under common Article 3 of the Geneva Conventions, and where the requisite elements are established, amounts to a war crime."
>
