---

## 1. Geopolitical Identity & Strategic Significance

North Darfur is the largest of Sudan's five Darfur states, bordering Chad to the west and Libya to the northwest. Its northern expanse encompasses trans-Saharan smuggling routes through the desert triangle, while its southern reaches encompass fertile mountainous zones in Jebel Marra. It has historically served as the epicenter of Darfur's political and ethnic governance.

---

## 2. Territorial Control History & Armed Presence

- **Historical Contestation**: Following the collapse of the 6th Infantry Division in [[El Fasher]] in late October 2025, the [[Rapid Support Forces]] consolidated territorial control across the state's urban and rural administrative centers (Kutum, Mellit, Kabkabiya).
- **Armed Pockets**: The Sudan Liberation Movement led by Abdul Wahid al-Nur (SLA-AW) maintains control over neutral zones around Tawila and the Jebel Marra massif, hosting hundreds of thousands of displaced non-Arab civilians.

---

## 3. Key Infrastructure, Trade Corridors & Resources

- **Trans-Saharan Trade Routes**: Highway corridors north to Kufra (Libya) and west into eastern Chad.
- **Agricultural & Pastoral Lands**: Vast seasonal grazing lands and rain-fed cereal farming corridors severely disrupted by conflict.
- **Artesian Water Wells & Oases**: Vital survival points in the desert belt.

---

## 4. Humanitarian Status, Displacement & Civil Services

North Darfur represents one of the world's most catastrophic humanitarian epicenters, with IPC Phase 5 Famine declared in Zamzam camp. Widespread denial of aid access by RSF forces has resulted in massive child mortality and malnutrition.
