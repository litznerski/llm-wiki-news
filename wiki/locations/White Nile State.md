# White Nile State

## 1. Geopolitical Identity & Strategic Significance

> [!NOTE] Strategic River Corridor, Energy Backbone & Southern Border Pivot
> **White Nile State** (`ولاية النيل الأبيض` / `SD16`), centered on its administrative capital Rabak and the commercial metropolis of Kosti, straddles the White Nile river corridor south of Khartoum, bordering South Sudan, Sennar, Al Jazirah, and North/South Kordofan. It hosts Sudan's largest thermal power generating station (Um Dabaker) and serves as the primary gateway for cross-border transit, fuel redistribution, and humanitarian barge corridors into South Sudan.

## 2. Territorial Control History & Armed Presence

White Nile State is a fiercely contested frontier. While the SAF 18th Infantry Division maintains headquarters in Kosti and secures Rabak, [[Ad Douiem]], and the eastern river bank, the [[Rapid Support Forces]] (RSF) have pushed incursions from western rural corridors and northern borders adjacent to Khartoum and Al Jazirah. RSF forces have mounted persistent drone offensives targeting vital energy infrastructure across the state, including the Mashkoor, Tandalti, and Um Dabaker power complexes.

## 3. Key Infrastructure, Trade Corridors & Resources

- **Um Dabaker Thermal Power Plant**: Sudan's largest thermal electricity generation station (located in Rabak), producing hundreds of megawatts feeding the national grid.
- **Mashkoor Transformer Substation**: Strategic regional substation distributing power northwards into Ad Douiem, El Kawa, and Wad az Zaki.
- **Kosti River Port & Railway**: Major riverine navigation hub and railway intersection linking central Sudan with Juba and South Sudanese oil fields.
- **Kenana & Asalaya Sugar Complexes**: Industrial agricultural complexes vital for domestic sugar production and agro-industrial employment.

## 4. Humanitarian Status, Displacement & Civil Services

- **Acute Displacement Hub**: Hosts extensive settlements of displaced populations from Khartoum, Gezira, and Kordofan.
- **Grid Collapse & Secondary Economic Shocks**: In late October 2026, simultaneous RSF drone strikes crippled the Mashkoor and Um Dabaker stations, throwing major municipalities into complete darkness, spiking food and ice prices, paralyzing hospitals, and severing water purification networks.
