# Red Sea State

> [!NOTE] State Geopolitical Anchor
> - **Canonical Name**: Red Sea State (`Wilayat al-Bahr al-Ahmar` / `ولاية البحر الأحمر`)
> - **Administrative Level**: `state_adm1` (`ADM1`), Federal Republic of Sudan
> - **UN OCHA P-Code**: `SD10`
> - **State Capital**: [[Port Sudan]] (`SD10001`) — De facto wartime administrative capital of Sudan.
> - **Current Control**: [[Sudanese Armed Forces]] (SAF) Controlled
> - **IPC Acute Food Insecurity**: **Phase 2 (Stressed)**, hosting hundreds of thousands of displaced persons from central and western Sudan.

---

## 1. Geopolitical Identity & Strategic Significance

Red Sea State borders Egypt to the north, Eritrea to the south, and River Nile and Kassala states to the west. As Sudan's sole maritime gateway on the Red Sea, it hosts the international port of [[Port Sudan]], the Bashayer crude oil export terminals, and the temporary wartime headquarters of the [[Transitional Sovereignty Council]], government ministries, foreign diplomatic missions, and United Nations agencies.

---

## 2. Territorial Control History & Armed Presence

- **Sovereign Base Area**: Completely controlled by the [[Sudanese Armed Forces]] and General Intelligence Service.
- **Deep-Strike Threats**: While ground frontlines remain hundreds of kilometers to the west and south, the state has experienced repeat long-range strike drone incursions targeting Port Sudan Airport, military bases, and energy nodes in [[Haiya]] (`SD10002`).

---

## 3. Key Infrastructure, Trade Corridors & Resources

- **Port Sudan Marine Terminals**: Handles the vast majority of Sudan's commercial maritime imports, humanitarian relief shipments, and fertilizer/grain imports.
- **Crude Oil Export Pipelines**: Terminus for the Greater Nile Oil Pipeline and Petrodar Pipeline carrying oil from South Sudan and Sudan.
- **Transportation Nodes**: Key junctions at [[Haiya]] connecting road and rail corridors into River Nile State, Kassala, and Gedaref.

---

## 4. Humanitarian Status, Displacement & Civil Services

Red Sea State has served as the primary sanctuary for civilians fleeing conflict in Khartoum, Al Jazirah, and Darfur. Public infrastructure and housing in Port Sudan operate far beyond capacity.
